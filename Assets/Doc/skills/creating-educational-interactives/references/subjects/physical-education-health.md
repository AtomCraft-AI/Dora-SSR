# Physical Education & Health Education

## Learning Actions

Recognize movement phases, sequence safe technique concepts, plan activity, compare strategies, understand basic health concepts, and reflect on habits or team decisions.

## Strong Patterns

- [Interactive visualization](@agent-skill/builtin/creating-educational-interactives/patterns/interactive-visualization.md) for movement phases/anatomical basics
- [System simulation](@agent-skill/builtin/creating-educational-interactives/patterns/system-simulation.md) for tactics, pacing, energy balance concepts
- [Dialogue role-play](@agent-skill/builtin/creating-educational-interactives/patterns/dialogue-roleplay.md) for safety/health decision scenarios

## Design Rules

- Keep movement instructions age-appropriate and conservative.
- Use animation/diagrams to show phases and key cues without pretending a 2D model can assess an individual's body accurately.
- Health scenarios should teach general principles and decision-making, not diagnose conditions or prescribe treatment.
- For sports tactics, let learners position players, choose options, and observe consequences.

## Completion Evidence

Correct sequencing, tactical explanation, safety decision, or general health concept application.

## Avoid

Medical diagnosis, extreme exercise prescriptions, shame-based body/weight scoring, or claiming simulated performance predicts real athletic ability.
