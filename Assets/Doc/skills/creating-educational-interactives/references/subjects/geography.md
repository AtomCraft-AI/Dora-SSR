# Geography

## Learning Actions

Read maps, manipulate layers, observe spatial patterns, model physical processes, compare regions, interpret data, and reason about human-environment systems.

## Strong Patterns

- [Map & timeline exploration](@agent-skill/builtin/creating-educational-interactives/patterns/map-timeline-exploration.md)
- [System simulation](@agent-skill/builtin/creating-educational-interactives/patterns/system-simulation.md)
- [Interactive visualization](@agent-skill/builtin/creating-educational-interactives/patterns/interactive-visualization.md)

## Design Rules

- Always make scale, legend, orientation, and units available when they matter.
- Link map changes to charts or process diagrams where useful.
- Physical geography simulations should expose process variables: slope, rainfall, temperature, pressure, plate motion, etc.
- Human geography tasks should reveal trade-offs and spatial constraints rather than reduce regions to stereotypes.

## Feedback

Ask learners to locate evidence on the map or explain a spatial pattern before revealing a conclusion.

## Avoid

Distorted maps presented as neutral measurements, unexplained color encodings, or deterministic claims that geography alone causes social outcomes.
